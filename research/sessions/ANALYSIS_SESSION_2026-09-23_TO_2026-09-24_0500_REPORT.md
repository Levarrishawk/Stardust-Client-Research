## Architecture consequence

The evidence continues to support a replacement organized around explicit platform/input and text-composition services, original-asset/resource adapters, a wire-compatible protocol/session layer, replicated object model, world/scene simulation, separate ground and space HUD compositions, and presentation/renderer backends. New HUD findings reinforce that original named UI pages and game-state/event routes are data contracts even when the modern interface is redesigned. Network-visible field widths stay as the server expects; the 64-bit process does **not** widen 32-bit wire fields or repack original asset records. Original assets remain mandatory. No engine choice was made; prototype host/DX12/Vulkan options should be revisited only after the user-requested comprehensive analysis gate is genuinely met.

## Major remaining unknowns and risks

1. **Original asset compatibility:** forward shader `IMPL/0009`/`PASS/0010` and some less common asset/error paths; exact CKAT numerical propagation and animation-message consumers; conditional broken APT/MESH reachability. These are material, not original-name-only questions.
2. **Breadth:** many UI, simulation, scene, renderer and helper functions remain unnamed or only partially understood. The audited 93,287 non-unwind Ghidra entries are an imperfect but sobering denominator; a 2,528-name registry is not function-level completion.
3. **Protocol behavior:** source-derived packet definitions and many client handlers are mapped, but action/reply timing, failure recovery, optional services, field semantics, and live interoperability are not thereby proven. The lottery `0x43C` directional mismatch is a concrete current-server profile gap.
4. **Reachability/ownership:** individual static paths may be dead, content-gated or version-specific. Constructor/callback linkage is stronger evidence than RTTI or nearby strings alone; many ownership and asynchronous lifecycle details still need closure.
