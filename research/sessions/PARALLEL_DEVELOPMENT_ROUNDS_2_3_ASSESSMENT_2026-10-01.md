## Recommendation

Retain the project lead and four persistent Low-reasoning specialists for rounds with independent boundaries. The lead owns acceptance, shared interfaces, integration and the final evidence record. Use fewer active specialists when the next question is one serial integration chain; do not invent extra research to occupy all four. A single-agent implementation pass remains appropriate for a shared-file/engine bottleneck. No Round4 is dispatched by this recommendation.

That breadth is useful. It does not establish a precise speedup over the old single-agent approach or a compute-cost saving.

## Comparison with the old approach

The prior comparison considered roughly47 minutes of single-agent work before Round1: four meaningful lighting/source/integration results were closed in that window. Those tasks differ from these milestones in complexity, implementation and validation burden. Neither counting milestones nor comparing lines/files gives an apples-to-apples benchmark. The preceding long session's51 recorded milestones over roughly13 hours is likewise unsuitable as a uniform unit of productivity.

Measured within the new structure, Round2's29m13s acceptance was faster than Round1's46m53s, while Round3 included a longer Runtime implementation/review path. Both later rounds accepted four distinct specialties without merge conflicts or widening production parser scope. This supports retaining the structure for this project stage, with moderate confidence; it does not prove every future round will outperform a single agent. No matched counterfactual run or reliable compute/cost telemetry was available. Parallel specialist effort can consume more aggregate model resources even when wall-clock completion improves.

## What worked and what should change

- Keep isolated checkouts, exact file ownership, verified dirty-workspace snapshots and one milestone per worker. All scope preflights passed; integration never required commits or discarded unrelated work.
- Keep the lead acceptance gate. Review corrected effect-graph assumptions, a palette-fallback negative fixture, portable reproduction instructions, omitted event textures/capture and whole-file newline normalization. Passing local assertions alone did not close visual acceptance.
- State the complete acceptance checklist before dispatch: original dependency loading, authored texture closure, negative cases, current-generation identity, owned teardown, visual capture and documentation. Review-driven scope corrections are part of delivery time.
- Preserve one GPU owner at a time. CPU/native work proceeds independently; shared Godot and full-scene validation belongs at integration. This host's single GPU remains a serial resource.
- Balance milestone sizes and dependencies. Networking/Rendering finished earlier than Runtime in Round3; the round barrier intentionally leaves them idle. Do not feed follow-on work without Levarris's authorization. Plan dependent work into a later accepted snapshot.
- Measure user-visible accepted behavior and end-to-end elapsed time. Keep research scores, registry coverage, implementation results and unknown compiled/live behavior separate.
